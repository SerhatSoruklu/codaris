## Workflow and scope findings

- Registration takes a required name, email, country, one of five role options, 20–2,000-character joining reason and password; image and profile URLs are optional. Registration is implemented locally in native C and PostgreSQL. The account service was not live at the time of this audit according to the repository release runbook and the owner-reported public API 404; recheck before publishing the notice.
- Successful email verification activates membership automatically. No staff application review, moderation UI or identity-verification process is implemented. Do not call mailbox verification identity verification or approval.
- Login accepts email or membership ID. Password reset/change is implemented; password changes revoke sessions. Logout removes the current server-side session.
- Account settings edit display name, country, role, external URLs and avatar. Email changes require the current password, invalidate verification and queue a new verification message. The original joining reason cannot be edited.
- No self-service account deletion endpoint exists. The repository runbook points deletion requests to the privacy mailbox, but no operational request-handling procedure is documented. Foreign-key cascades exist for related rows if an account is actually removed by an administrator; this does not establish a complete deletion process or backup erasure behavior.
- The mail worker performs cleanup when it runs. Do not describe those schedules as live until the service/timer is installed and monitored.
- The contact form uses the fixed `admin@coupyn.com` destination, permits 15 fixed topics, validates fields server-side, checks exact same-origin JSON POSTs, and limits submissions per request peer and normalized email. Content is secretbox-encrypted in a separate PostgreSQL outbox while delivery is pending; SMTP receives an admin notification with the validated sender as Reply-To and a receipt to the sender. The configured CODARIS sender is used for both; no no-reply alias is assumed. The outbox is removed after both sends are accepted or after 30 days. Actual mail account access, SMTP provider and mailbox retention remain unverified.
- No age field or age validation, payment/subscription, AI processing, profiling, automated membership decision, public member directory, chat or working staff review feature was found in the current code.
- Profile links are checked/normalized as HTTPS claims. External ownership is not verified. Image upload is transformed by the browser into pixels, and the server accepts only the fixed pixel buffer.
- Public community output consists of counts of email-verified accounts and distinct normalized self-reported countries. It is aggregate output, not a profile directory.
- Analytics/advertising libraries, tracking cookies, localStorage and sessionStorage were not found in the reviewed runtime files. This is a repository audit, not a scan of a live browser/network session.

## Legal source checks

Current ICO guidance says privacy information must identify purposes, lawful bases, recipients, transfers, retention and rights, and must be clear and accessible. ICO guidance on PECR says necessary-service cookie exemptions are narrow and information about cookies is still good practice. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 data-protection complaint provisions are in force as of June 2026; the ICO says organisations need a clear complaint route, acknowledgement within 30 days, investigation and an outcome. See the ICO’s [right-to-be-informed guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-be-informed/), [cookie guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/), and [DUAA overview](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-what-does-it-mean-for-organisations/). This audit is not legal advice or a substitute for the operator’s legal review.
